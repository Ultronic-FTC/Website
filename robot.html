Our Engineering Design Process

Innovation is in our DNA. From brainstorming to CAD to iteration, every Ultronic robot embodies precision, teamwork, and creativity. We're currently ranked in the Top 10% Globally for our engineering prowess.

01

Brainstorm & Design

We kick off each season by deconstructing the game challenge, identifying key scoring strategies, and sketching initial concepts to define our robot's core architecture.

02

CAD & Prototyping

Every idea is brought to life in CAD, allowing us to validate geometry, test integration, and catch potential issues before a single part is machined. Prototypes follow to test mechanisms in the real world.

03

Build & Test

With a solid design, we move to fabrication and assembly. Each subsystem undergoes rigorous testing to evaluate performance, reliability, and consistency under competition pressure.

04

Iterate & Refine

Failure is a data point. We analyze test results, driver feedback, and competition data to continuously improve our systems, making our robot faster, stronger, and more reliable.

Meet 1 Overview


Following our separation from a world-renouned FTC team, Ultronic was left with under three weeks to align the stars. Not only did we have to design, build, and program a competition-ready robot from the ground up, but to also set up outreach events, line up sponsors, begin building a reputation both inside and outside of the FIRST community, and get our team structure in order.


After vehemently working on our robot for 11 days, we felt that our robot design was a little too ambitious for the time we had remaining. So, we shifted our focus to a simpler design, thus the advent of our first competition robot: Groot.


Over the course of three days, we designed, iterated, tested, programmed, and practiced driving Groot to achieve the highest success possible so that we were able to compete with those who have been working on their robots for months. We ended up placing in the top 10% among all FTC teams globally.

Meet 2 Overview


After our success in Meet 1, we decided to continue developing Groot. We made minor but key improvements to the design that pushed us even higher in the rankings. We lowered the intake to have a more reliable compression on the balls, added a gate that held the balls before the turret, and refined the turret system to be capable of 180 degrees of real-time goal tracking.


On the programming side, we rebuilt the turret subsystem with a quadrature encoder and odometry tracking for precise positioning. We implemented auto distance velocity calculations for dynamic shooting, allowing the flywheel to adjust speed based on distance to the goal. The Limelight vision system was fully integrated for real-time target tracking, and we transitioned to a command-based architecture using WPILIB for cleaner, more maintainable code.

Meet 3 Overview


Meet 3 was filled with excitement as we were testing out our first iteration of HAWKEYE. Knowing there was a risk that the subsystems wouldn't be reliable enough to last through a match, we came up with a plan B: Defense. We spent hours watching videos and developing a defensive strategy in which we wouldn't score any balls, only prevent the other alliance from scoring.


On competition day, we had our systems working, but they consistently jammed and we didn't want to risk damaging our robot. So we fully pivoted to our defensive strategy, which was so successful that we climbed to 7th place in the region. For this meet, we owe our drive team a special thanks for their exceptional adaptability and execution under pressure.


On the programming side, we built out an initial dashboard with subsystem simulators for faster iteration. The turret received closed-loop control with analog feedback for improved accuracy. We completely redesigned the spindexer motor control and ball detection system, and added an RGB indicator light to display the current ball count to our drivers.

League Championship Overview


League Championships showcased the culmination of months of iteration on HAWKEYE. We finally got our spindexer working reliably, allowing for consistent ball indexing and shooting. The new turret system was dialed in, and we achieved a reliable autonomous routine with auto park capabilities. Our drivers also developed effective zoning strategies to control the field.


We were honored to receive the Inspire Award, the most prestigious award in FIRST Tech Challenge. This award recognizes teams that embody the true spirit of FIRST, demonstrating excellence in robot design, team spirit, and gracious professionalism. Earning this recognition is a testament to the dedication and passion of every member of Ultronic.
